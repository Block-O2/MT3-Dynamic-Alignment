## Conditions and success definition

The code uses a Franka Panda and a box translated along a reversing semicircle of radius 0.15 m. A static grasp is generated and recorded inside PyBullet. The loop runs at 30 Hz with eight physics substeps per control step, up to 25 seconds and three grasp attempts per trial. No original MT3 hardware demonstrations, learned policy, or policy retraining are used.

| Condition | Implementation in the current script |
|---|---|
| `static_replay` | Original demo poses; no online object transform |
| `raw_observation` | Centroid translation; no Kalman filter or prediction; freezes demo time on invalid clouds |
| `dynamic_tau0` | Kalman tracking, **`tau=0.1 s`**, lateral-error adaptive replay; the historical label does not mean zero latency compensation |
| `uncertainty_gated` | Same tracking and prediction; substitutes position covariance magnitude for lateral error in the adaptive gate |
| `oracle_pose` | Ground-truth box translation; normal demo clock |

Dropout replaces an entire observed point cloud with an empty cloud with probability 0.15 on each observation call in the perception-based conditions. This models random missing observations, not arm-induced occlusion or measured camera latency. PCA yaw estimation is disabled for this grasping experiment.

Success requires **all** of: final lift >50 mm; horizontal error to the object-relative demo target ≤15 mm at attachment; gripper-axis orientation error ≤20°; maximum approach error ≤20 mm in the final 0.5 seconds before attachment. The box attaches using a fixed PyBullet constraint when proximity and gripper thresholds are met. This evaluates geometric alignment and replay, not frictional grasp stability or force control.

## Interpretation and limits

- Static and raw-observation replay fail in these recorded settings. Dynamic replay performs better, but filtering, prediction and replay timing vary together; this is not an isolated causal test of filtering or latency compensation.
- The uncertainty gate scores below dynamic replay at 2/4/6 cm/s in both runs. At 8 cm/s its observed rate is slightly higher. The data do not establish equivalence or a general benefit of covariance gating; no covariance trace is archived in the trial CSV.
- Oracle pose reaches 20/20 in every cell. Perfect translation enables success in this simplified scene; it does not rule out controller limitations under real perception, delay or contact dynamics.
- In May 28 dynamic replay, failures are `attempt_limit`/`approach` = 5/1 at 2 cm/s, and `attempt_limit` = 15 at 8 cm/s. May 24 also has 14 `attempt_limit` failures at 8 cm/s. The earlier claim that lift misses dominate high-speed dynamic replay is inconsistent with the raw failure labels.
- The archived chart's “weak velocity signal,” “optimal range,” and “control bandwidth limit” annotations are hypotheses. The counts alone cannot establish those mechanisms or a universally optimal speed.
- Twenty trials at one seed give coarse, uncertain estimates. A Bernoulli outcome standard deviation of about 40–50 percentage points is not an uncertainty interval on the estimated success rate. No statistical significance or causal benefit of dropout is claimed.
- Current simulation does not inject a measured end-to-end latency or compare controlled `tau` values. Synthetic latency illustrations and interface tests do not establish real latency-compensation performance.
- The tracker is planar SE(2); this experiment tests translation only and one grasping task. Sawyer/RealSense integration and all real-robot behavior remain unvalidated.
